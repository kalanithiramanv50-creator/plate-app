Plate Privacy Policy

Effective 11 October 2026 · Applies to the Plate calorie tracker app for Android

In short: Plate has no accounts, no ads and no tracking. Your food diary and body details stay on your phone. Only the words you search for, and barcodes you scan, leave your phone, so we can look up the food.

Information stored only on your phone

Plate saves the following on your device. It is never sent to us or anyone else:

  • Your food diary: foods, amounts, meals, dates and times
  • Your goals: calorie goal, macro split, and the TDEE you enter
  • Your profile, if you add it: name (optional), sex, age, height, weight and activity level

You can change these in the app at any time. Uninstalling Plate, or clearing its storage in Android Settings, deletes all of it.

Information sent to look up food

  • Food searches. When you search, the text you type is sent to Plate's food server (hosted by Vercel), which looks the food up in USDA FoodData Central. It is also sent directly to Open Food Facts. Our server does not store your searches or link them to you; results are cached by search text only.
  • Barcode scans. Only the barcode number is sent to Open Food Facts to find the product.

As with any internet request, these services receive your device's IP address. Our hosting provider may keep short-lived technical logs to run and protect the service. Open Food Facts and USDA handle requests under their own privacy policies. All requests use encrypted connections (HTTPS).

Camera

Plate asks for camera access only to scan barcodes, and uses the camera only while the scanner screen is open. Camera images are never saved or sent anywhere.

What we don't do

  • No sign-up or account
  • No advertising, and no advertising or analytics SDKs
  • No selling or sharing of personal information
  • No location access

Children

Plate is not directed at children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect information from children.

Health information

Calorie and nutrition figures are estimates for general wellness, not medical advice. Talk to a doctor or dietitian before making big changes to your diet.

Changes

If this policy changes, we will update this page and its effective date.

Contact
